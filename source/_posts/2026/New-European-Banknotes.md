In 2025, the number of people in Germany paying by credit or debit card overtook the number of cash payers for the first time. At first glance, it seems that cash (coins and banknotes) will eventually become completely obsolete here too. I've personally noticed this: I pretty much pay for everything via my smartphone now and hardly ever carry any cash with me. It's so convenient, and when abroad, you don't even have to go through the hassle of exchanging money.

Many other European countries are already much further along in this process. Sweden, for example, moved almost entirely away from cash nearly 10 years ago … BUT … they are now realising the downsides of a purely digital payment system, such as a lack of financial inclusion, cyberattacks and fading trust in international financial partners … and are turning back the clock! **Back to Cash!**

It is therefore only logical that, while investing in its own digital euro solutions, the EU is simultaneously addressing the replacement of the ageing euro banknotes. These efforts, which have been underway since 2023 in the form of a [design contest](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/press/pubbydate/2026/html/ecb.desreportbanknotes202607), have culminated this year in a [shortlist of 10 new designs for the future banknotes](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/euro/banknotes/future_banknotes/html/design-proposals.en.html), which are to be assessed by the end of the year before a winner is chosen.

In the run-up to the competition, the ECB defined two different [groups of motifs](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/press/pr/date/2025/html/ecb.pr250131~611055a567.de.html). The first is **European Culture**, represented on the front by various portraits of well-known Europeans such as Maria Callas, Ludwig van Beethoven and Leonardo da Vinci, and on the back by groups of people. The second is the **Rivers and Birds** group, featuring on the front and European buildings on the back. Five designs have been shortlisted for each motif group. All European citizens will now be involved in the evaluation of the shortlist via a survey on the shortlisted designs; however, a great many other people are also likely to take part, as there are, quite rightly, no restrictions on access.

Personally, I don't really understand the motif groups, nor do I get some of the designs, but it's worth noting that some of them only work when held upright - which I'd see as a real improvement, because after all, we do hold these notes upright in our hands when counting them out. The landscape format may be traditional, but it's impractical. I'm also not keen on having people on the notes and hope the EU doesn't introduce this now, having relied on architectural landmarks for decades. I don't need the Callas in my wallet, nor do I need Bertha von Suttner - even though I might recognise their life's work if I'd actually engaged with it. At least it's not politicians or other statesmen or stateswomen. Dear US friends: When are you going to swap George Washington's portrait for The Donald's again?

My personal favourite is definitely **Design J**, closely followed by **Design I** and **D**. Something with birds ...
