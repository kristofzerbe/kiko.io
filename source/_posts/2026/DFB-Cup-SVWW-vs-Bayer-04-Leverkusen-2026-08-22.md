It’s been a while since I wrote my last post about a match involving my favourite club, SV Wehen Wiesbaden. I’m not quite sure why, but I promise to do better, as I still have a season ticket and try to watch every home match. I’ve even convinced my wife. She has one now too ;)

The start in the new 3. Bundesliga season wasn’t exactly thrilling. We lost our first home match against [**Preußen Münster**](https://www.kicker.de/wiesbaden-gegen-muenster-2026-liga-5233214/analyse) **1–3** right off the bat. I was looking forward to the match because my favourite player from last season, [Ryan Johanson](https://www.kicker.de/ryan-johansson/spieler/3-liga/2025-26/sv-wehen-wiesbaden), had transferred to Münster and immediately had to return to Wiesbaden for the very first match. 
The second match, away against [**Würzburg**](https://www.kicker.de/wuerzburg-gegen-wiesbaden-2026-liga-5233230/analyse), was unfortunately also a disaster. The final score was even 4–0.

Today, however, there was a break in league play, and the first round of the DFB Cup was played. In the pairing draw, we immediately drew a tough opponent: the 2023 German football champions, [**Bayer 04 Leverkusen**](https://www.kicker.de/bayer-04-leverkusen/info/)! Since the Cup is known to have its own rules, and we put up a really good and “close” Cup match against Bayern Munich last year, some of us were feeling a little hopeful.

<!-- more -->

---

In the first half, it certainly looked as though we could give Bayer a hard time. The boys ran and fought just as all the fans would actually like to see them do in the league, and managed to get into the opposition's penalty area on a few times, but the Leverkusen defence was always on hand and the two or three standards we won were wasted. We weren’t playing great, but we were in good heart until the 43rd minute, when Leverkusen scored **0:1** through Patrik Schick.

Probably boosted by the goal just before half-time and the manager’s occasional reminders to his players, the second half started as the first had ended… **0:2**. Leverkusen hit their operating temperature and the difference in class was now clearly evident. We kept up with them, but lacked precision in our passing, pace and finesse. After the **0:3** and the **0:4**, it was clear to everyone that, unfortunately, there would be no surprise today.

I’m curious to see how the team reacts to these three clear defeats. Next week we’re at home in the league against Rot-Weiß Essen, and a bit of fire in the belly would be just the thing.
