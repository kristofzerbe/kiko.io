[Gdańsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gda%C5%84sk) has an eventful [history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Gda%C5%84sk), and that was one of the reasons why my loved one and I were drawn to this area for our planned summer vacation on the Polish Baltic Sea. And we weren't disappointed. The city's charm is unmatched, and you can feel and breathe the past centuries at every turn. From the first traces of settlement in the 9th century, through Pomerania's shifting reigns between Polish, Danish, and German rulers, its membership in the Hanseatic League, the Republic of Gdańsk, the Free City of Danzig, all the way to the German Wehrmacht's raid on a Polish ammunition depot on the [Westerplatte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westerplatte) peninsula just outside the city in August 1939 (which marks the beginning of World War II) and its integration into the Republic of Poland.

## The Harbour / Westerplatte

The city itself isn't located directly on the Baltic Sea, but rather about 8 kilometers upstream from the Westerplatte Peninsula along a tributary of the Vistula River. The entire stretch is one big harbour, and as a tourist, you simply can't miss hopping on one of the sightseeing boats to take it all in.
