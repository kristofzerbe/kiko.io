When Robert Birming, following [Junited](https://robertbirming.com/junited-2026-blog-sharing/) and [JulyReply](https://robertbirming.com/julyreply-2026-blog-connecting/), called for contributions to [**AuguStory**](https://robertbirming.com/augustory-story-blog/) under the heading '*The Story of your Blog*', I thought to myself: If there's one thing I can do, it's write about my blog … but I found it much harder than I'd expected, simply because I've written about it so many times before.

<!-- more -->
 
When I made a fresh start with blogging on kiko.io in September 2019 – initially just to publicly document technical matters for my own benefit – my very second post was about the creation of the blog itself. This turned into a whole series of posts called [**A New Blog**](/series/a-new-blog/), under which I have published 37 posts to date. Whenever I learnt something new about my current static site generator of choice, [Hexo](https://hexo.io), adapted it to my needs or implemented something entirely new, I wrote a post about it. In addition, last year I began compiling the history, methodology and technology behind this blog in a [**Colophon**](/colophon/). It currently consists of the sections [Impetus & Technology](https://kiko.io/post/Colophon-Impetus-Technology/), [Hosting & Deployment](https://kiko.io/post/Colophon-Hosting-Deployment/), [Writing](https://kiko.io/post/Colophon-Writing/) and [Special Pages](https://kiko.io/post/Colophon-Special-Pages/). There are three more I'd want to write: Imaging, Styling and Integrations & Privacy.

---

For me, the focus of my blog has shifted somewhat in the meantime. Whereas the claim/slogan at the start was 'Memorable Tech Stuff', the 'Tech' has been in brackets for some time now, as there isn't much left from my actual job as a software developer that I could write about, because I've moved completely into management and only code for my own enjoyment these days… as I'm doing for this website, for example. Without code, it simply doesn't work – even though I'm giving more and more space to my second great passion: photography. In my spare time, I'll soon be spending more time in Lightroom than in Visual Studio Code, or out and about with my camera to get more material. You can see this reflected in my posts too; the article series [Thomas Garden](/series/thomas-garden) and [New Photos](/series/new-photos) now contain 46 posts, and I'm currently working on more photos and, in turn, more posts.

As a big fan of the [IndieWeb](https://indieweb.org), means not relying on large, commercial providers to publish own thoughts, but rather building own things like a skilled craftsman, the aspect of syndicating my own content according to the [POSSE principle](https://indieweb.org/POSSE) is something I'm particularly dedicated to. Every article, every note and every photo has its own page on kiko.io, which includes a list of syndications on other platforms, primarily in the Fediverse. All interactions with these syndications, together with the webmentions posted by other blogs, are displayed on the respective page via the JavaScript solution [Mentions United](https://github.com/kristofzerbe/Mentions-United). Actually I post new syndications several times a week. At the moment, for example, I post one daily on Mastodon, where I'm contributing my photos to the hashtag campaign [#Fotovorschlag](https://indieweb.social/@FotoVorschlag@mastodon.social) (PhotoSuggestion).

Right now, I'm finding it a bit difficult to write posts, because to produce a good text, you need not only time for basic research, but also a reasonably clear head. When I sit down to write a new piece, for almost a year now, I've been writing it in my mother tongue German first, then running it through the DeepL translator and adapting the result to my sense of the English language, as I've noticed that posts I write directly in English tend to come across as rather stilted. I certainly don't ditch the original German text afterwards, but attach it to the article as the German version. It's a win-win situation for readers.

---

## Closing Thoughts

It's great fun to have a little corner of the internet of your own where you can do whatever you like. It doesn't really matter whether or who reads it, even though I'm happy about every LIKE and every REPOST or even a REPLY, because when you do something, whatever it is, positive feedback is a welcome form of acknowledgement. However, the line between LIKE-hunting is seductively thin, and you have to be careful that the dopamine rushes don't make you dizzy. But nobody can tell me they run a blog just to keep it from being seen. It's a conscious engagement with the public and helps to build networks that go far beyond what was possible in the past. I think a relaxed attitude towards this is essential. However, I'm technically capable of implementing my ideas and visions almost exactly as I envision them, and I understand those who prefer to rely on a ready-made platform so as not to get frustrated with the code right from the start. From this perspective, I don't think AI-based approaches are entirely unreasonable, even though I tend to belong to the sceptical faction.

Happy Blogging :)
