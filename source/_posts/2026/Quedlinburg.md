A holiday road trip of over 1,100 kilometers by car to our beautiful neighboring country, Poland, is something you're better off doing with a stopover at our age (50+), because the main point is to get some downtime, and also because that way we could pick up a few nice impressions of other places along the way.

So, this summer, on our way to Gdańsk, we stopped in [Potsdam](/post/Potsdam), and on the way back, we planned to visit [**Quedlinburg**](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quedlinburg), since we'd already read a lot about the charm of the old imperial residence and it was on our bucket list anyway.

<!-- more -->

This town in the Harz Mountains, which was granted city rights in 994 A.D. and served as a residence for kings, emperors, and the Church for many centuries, made it onto the UNESCO World Heritage List exactly 1,000 years later thanks to its architectural beauty. But there was also a fair amount of luck involved, because for 40 years under the socialist GDR regime, the city resisted efforts to tear down the old buildings and replace them with prefabricated concrete buildings. . . simply because there wasn't enough money to do so. With the help of many Polish craftsmen, the city's historic buildings were restored after German reunification, and today it is a true gem that attracts many tourists to this otherwise secluded area.
